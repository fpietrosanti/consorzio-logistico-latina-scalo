## Post 1

Consorzio Logistico Latina Scalo operates a third-party logistics site in Via della Dinamica, in the Tor Tre Ponti area of Latina Scalo (province of Latina), about 70 km by road south of Rome EUR. The site has 19,000 square metres of covered warehousing and 25,000 square metres of open yards, in modular buildings of 500 to 3,000 square metres with a clear height of up to 10 metres.

For companies based outside Italy, the location places stock on the Tyrrhenian corridor between Rome and Naples. Latina railway station, on the Rome–Formia–Naples line, is in Latina Scalo. The SS148 Pontina connects the area with Rome, and the SS156 Monti Lepini road links Latina to Frosinone and the A1 motorway. Rome Fiumicino airport is about 90 km by road and the port of Gaeta about 70 km.

The Consortium brings together five local companies with more than fifty years of combined experience in storage, handling and road transport. According to the Consortium, the site operates 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

## Post 2

Companies outside Italy that supply Italian customers can entrust storage, order preparation and delivery to a single operator. At its site in Latina Scalo, Consorzio Logistico Latina Scalo provides third-party logistics (3PL) with one point of contact for the client.

The service covers goods receipt with quantity and quality checks, racked and floor storage, picking, packing, labelling and palletising, and road transport with a dedicated fleet for B2B deliveries across Italy and last-mile distribution in Lazio. Inventory is managed through a warehouse management system (WMS). Transport documents, including Italian delivery notes (DDT), and goods traceability are handled on site, with integration into the client's management systems.

Costs are variable and linked to actual volumes, an arrangement that suits companies building up their Italian sales before committing to a warehouse of their own. For a quote, the Consortium asks for the type of goods, the pallet spaces or square metres required, the expected duration and any additional services.

## Post 3

Online sellers based in the UK, Ireland or elsewhere outside Italy who ship every Italian order from their home country add a cross-border leg to each parcel. Holding stock in Italy moves that leg upstream: goods travel in bulk to an Italian warehouse and individual orders leave from there.

From Latina Scalo, Rome EUR is about 70 km by road, Pomezia about 50 km and Aprilia about 34 km. Consorzio Logistico Latina Scalo stores goods on racking and on the floor, prepares orders with picking, packing and labelling, and delivers with its own fleet in Lazio, alongside B2B deliveries across Italy. Stock is tracked in a WMS and the transport documentation required in Italy is issued on site.

The Consortium's analysis of e-commerce fulfilment in Lazio describes how the service is organised, which data a seller needs to prepare and how storage and handling costs are calculated.

## Post 4

Three ports on the Lazio coast belong to the same port authority: Civitavecchia, Fiumicino and Gaeta form the system of the Central-Northern Tyrrhenian Sea Port System Authority. In 2025 the system handled more than 13 million tonnes of goods. Civitavecchia, the main port of Lazio, handles Ro-Ro traffic, motorways of the sea, automotive and refrigerated agri-food cargo. Gaeta handled about 1.8 million tonnes in 2025, mostly bulk.

The three ports are included in the Simplified Logistics Zone (ZLS) of Lazio, a regime established by decree of 17 November 2025 that covers 64 municipalities. It provides simplified administrative procedures and a dedicated one-stop shop; in areas eligible for regional aid, investments in capital goods qualify for a tax credit extended to 2026–2028.

Gaeta is about 70 km by road from the Consortium's site in Latina Scalo. Goods landed at the Tyrrhenian ports can be stored, handled and redistributed by road from Latina Scalo to customers in Lazio and across Italy.
