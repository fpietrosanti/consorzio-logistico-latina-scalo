An SME that entrusts its warehousing and shipping to a third-party logistics operator (3PL) signs a contract that has no single body of rules in Italy. Practice builds it by combining the rules on service contracts, deposit and carriage. The practical value of the contract therefore lies in the clauses written by the parties: what the operator does, how the service is measured, who is liable for what and how the relationship can be ended.

According to the "Gino Marchet" Contract Logistics Observatory of the Politecnico di Milano, cited by Shipping Italy on 13 November 2025, the turnover of third-party logistics in Italy was estimated at 112.4 billion euros in 2025, with about 79,000 companies active in 2023.

## The legal framework

The main reference is the **service contract** (appalto di servizi, Art. 1655 of the Civil Code), in which the operator organises equipment and staff and bears the business risk. The rules on **deposit** (Arts. 1766 et seq. of the Civil Code) apply to the safekeeping of goods, including the duty of care under Art. 1768.

Since 2022 the Civil Code has contained a specific provision. **Article 1677-bis**, introduced by Law No 234 of 30 December 2021 (Art. 1(819)) and amended by Decree-Law No 36 of 30 April 2022, converted by Law 79/2022, provides that where the contract covers two or more logistics services (receiving, processing, deposit, safekeeping, dispatch, transfer and distribution of goods belonging to others), the rules on the contract of carriage apply, insofar as compatible, to the activities of moving goods. For the transport leg, this also brings in the limit of compensation under Art. 1696 of the Civil Code: 1 euro per kilogram of gross weight in domestic carriage, unless otherwise agreed.

Employment law aspects, starting with joint liability in service contracts under Art. 29 of Legislative Decree 276/2003, should be assessed with a labour consultant, especially when the operator uses subcontractors.

## Subject matter and scope of services

The first clause describes the activities included, one by one, with their start and end points. A typical list:

- receiving goods with a quantity check and, where provided for, a sample quality check;
- storage in racking or on the floor, stating the unit of measurement (pallet space or square metre);
- order picking by full pallet, carton or item;
- [value-added services]({{< relref "/servizi/lavorazioni-valore-aggiunto" >}}): labelling, kitting, repacking;
- dispatch and [transport]({{< relref "/servizi/trasporto" >}}), direct or through third-party carriers;
- [document management]({{< relref "/servizi/gestione-documentale" >}}): DDTs (Italian delivery notes), reporting, interfaces with the client's management system;
- returns management.

Anything not listed is an extra and should be priced separately. A technical annex with forecast volumes (average and peak stock, order lines per day, seasonality) is used to size the service and to establish when a change in volumes allows rates to be revised.

## Stock counts and stock discrepancies

The contract must state how and when goods are counted. The most common methods are the **annual physical stocktake**, with operations suspended, and **cycle counting**, which counts part of the locations each day according to a schedule, so that every item is counted several times a year without stopping the warehouse.

Stock discrepancies are handled through three elements:

1. a **tolerance**, expressed as a percentage of the value or of the units handled in the period, within which discrepancies are not charged;
2. an **offsetting rule** between surpluses and shortages in the same period;
3. a **compensation value** for shortages above the threshold, normally the documented purchase cost rather than the selling price.

The contract should specify who bears the cost of extraordinary stock counts requested by the client. The Civil Code also grants the depositary a lien on the goods deposited for claims arising from the deposit (Art. 2761 of the Civil Code): a clause on redelivery arrangements in the event of disputes prevents goods from being held up.

## Service indicators

An indicator (KPI) has contractual value only if the formula, data source, measurement period and threshold are defined. The most widely used:

| Indicator | Formula | Data source |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory accuracy | locations or items without discrepancies / locations or items counted × 100 | cycle counts and annual stocktakes |
| Orders fulfilled by the cut-off | orders shipped on the scheduled day / orders received before the cut-off × 100 | WMS |
| OTIF (On Time In Full) | on-time and complete deliveries / total deliveries × 100 | carrier's proofs of delivery |
| Dock-to-stock time | hours between unloading and system availability of the goods | WMS |
| Picking accuracy | error-free order lines / order lines shipped × 100 | complaints and outbound checks |
| Damage rate | damaged cartons / cartons handled × 100 | non-conformity reports |

For OTIF, it should be clarified who is liable for delay when delivery is carried out by a carrier chosen by the client. For the cut-off, the exact time should be stated together with what happens to orders received afterwards. The data must be extractable from the operator's WMS (warehouse management system) and made available to the client at a set frequency, for example monthly.

## Penalties and incentives

Penalties link the price to the service level. The **penalty clause** is governed by Articles 1382 et seq. of the Civil Code, and Art. 1384 allows the court to reduce it if manifestly excessive. In practice, penalties are linked to deviations measured monthly or quarterly (for example inventory accuracy below the agreed threshold), with an annual cap expressed as a percentage of the service turnover. Some contracts also provide a symmetrical bonus mechanism for results above the threshold.

## Liability and insurance

The liability clause distinguishes three areas:

- **safekeeping in the warehouse**: the operator is liable for damage caused by its own fault, often with a limit per kilogram or per event;
- **transport**: the rules on carriage and the related limits apply, under Art. 1677-bis;
- **processing**: liability for labelling or packing errors is governed by the rules on service contracts.

The contract states which policies each party takes out, with limits and beneficiaries, and whether the client's goods are insured at full value. If the operator processes personal data on behalf of the client, for example the addresses of end customers, it must be appointed as data processor under Art. 28 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.

## Duration, termination and exit

According to the Contract Logistics Observatory, the average duration of warehousing contracts in Italy fell from 3.4 to 3 years over the period observed since 2017; that of strategic outsourcing contracts from 3.8 to 3.4 years, and that of transport contracts from 1.7 to 1.6 years. A longer duration allows the operator to amortise start-up costs, but should be balanced by a termination clause for repeated failure to meet the indicators.

The exit clause (exit plan) sets the notice period, a final joint stocktake, the handover of historical data in a readable format and the arrangements for transferring the goods to the new warehouse.
