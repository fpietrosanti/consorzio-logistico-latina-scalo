Serving the southern quadrant of Rome from a warehouse outside the Grande Raccordo Anulare (GRA, Rome's orbital motorway) means adding an outbound and return transfer to every delivery round. The starting point is therefore road distance. From the Via della Dinamica site in Latina Scalo (LT), the OSRM route calculation for cars in normal traffic gives about 70 kilometres and about 70 minutes to EUR, about 55 kilometres to Santa Palomba and about 50 kilometres to Pomezia.

The second factor is the rules on access to the city for goods vehicles, which in Rome operate on several levels and in 2026 were the subject of a trial scheme and of litigation.

Two routes divide the quadrant. The western areas (EUR, Torrino, Spinaceto, Magliana) are reached via the SS148 Pontina state road, which between Rome and Borgo Piave has two separate carriageways with two lanes in each direction; on the section between Pomezia and Latina north the speed limit has been lowered from 110 to 80 km/h. The eastern areas (Morena, Anagnina, Tor Vergata) require routes through the Castelli Romani road network or on the GRA, with longer times for the same distance, as shown by the 70 minutes estimated for Morena against 53 kilometres.

For vehicles over 3.5 tonnes the Italian Highway Code (Art. 142) sets lower speed limits than for cars: 80 km/h on main non-urban roads and 70 km/h on secondary roads.

## Access rules: four levels

### Fascia Verde

The **Fascia Verde** (green belt) coincides with the area inside the GRA. According to the summary published by Sicurauto on 19 January 2026, it is in force Monday to Saturday, excluding mid-week public holidays, and bans the most polluting vehicles; on days of high pollution the ban is progressively extended to other classes, up to Euro 6 diesels. For a fleet working in Rome, the vehicles' emission class is therefore an operational requirement, not just a tax matter.

### Goods vehicle ZTL and VAM ZTL

The limited traffic zones (ZTL) of the centre (Centro Storico, Tridente, Trastevere) have separate rules for cars and for goods vehicles. According to the same Sicurauto summary:

- the **VAM ZTL** is closed to vehicles over 7.5 metres long every day from 05:00 to midnight;
- in the **goods vehicle ZTL** (ZTL Merci), vehicles up to 35 quintals of Euro 4-6 class are banned from 17:30 to 20:00;
- heavier vehicles, and electric, LPG, methane or hybrid vehicles over 65 quintals, are banned from 07:00 to 20:00;
- petrol and diesel lorries from Euro 0 to Euro 3 cannot enter.

Access requires a permit, issued and paid for through the services of Roma Servizi per la Mobilità.

### The 2026 trial scheme for the historic centre

By mayoral ordinance No 105 of 31 July 2026, Roma Capitale introduced, on a trial basis from 7 September to 7 December 2026, a ban on access from 08:00 to 18:00 for goods vehicles in ZTL A1 (Tridente) and neighbouring areas, even for vehicles with a permit. According to Sicurauto (3 August 2026), vehicles already inside the perimeter could operate until 09:30 and those carrying temperature-controlled foodstuffs until 10:00, with exemptions for medicines, authorised construction sites, removals and emergency services.

Between 2 and 4 September the Lazio Regional Administrative Court (TAR) granted the requests for interim suspension filed by express couriers and by a food and beverage delivery company, suspending the measure for the applicants pending the full hearing set for 23 September 2026. The outcome of that hearing had not been verified at the date of this article: anyone planning deliveries in the Tridente should check the status of the measure on the Roma Mobilità website.

### Changes announced for 2027

Roma Capitale's councillor for mobility, **Eugenio Patanè**, announced in August 2026 that electronic gates would also be activated on exit from the ZTLs, starting with the Centro Storico, with a target date of 7 January 2027, and that deliveries would be divided into time bands by category of goods, according to perishability and urgency. Sensors are planned in loading and unloading bays, linked to a booking system for accredited vehicles. These are announced measures, to be verified when they come into force.

## Delivery windows and round planning

With a transfer of about one hour each way, organising the delivery round becomes a matter of timing. Four constraints have to be reconciled:

- the consignees' **receiving windows**: shops and offices generally receive in the morning, while retail distribution centres work on booked slots;
- the **restriction periods** of the goods vehicle ZTL and the Fascia Verde, which vary by vehicle weight and class;
- the **driving and rest times** under Regulation (EC) 561/2006 for vehicles over 3.5 tonnes: a maximum of 9 hours' daily driving, extendable to 10 twice a week, and a 45-minute break after 4 hours 30 minutes of driving;
- the **delivery density** per round, which determines the cost per delivery: more stops concentrated in the same area reduce the weight of the transfer.

In practice two models are combined. The first is a dawn departure with vehicles loaded the evening before, to reach the city before the restriction periods and peak traffic. The second is **break-bulk**: a heavy vehicle transfers the goods to a transit point closer to the city, from which light or electric vans make the deliveries; this is the [cross-docking]({{< relref "/approfondimenti/cross-docking-quando-conviene" >}}) model.

## The Latina Scalo site

Consorzio Logistico Latina Scalo operates in Via della Dinamica, Latina Scalo (LT), with 19,000 square metres of covered space, 25,000 square metres of open areas and yards for manoeuvring articulated lorries. The [transport]({{< relref "/servizi/trasporto" >}}) service includes road distribution with a dedicated fleet and last-mile delivery; the warehouse also handles [cross-docking]({{< relref "/servizi/cross-docking" >}}). For the areas served, see the pages on [logistics in Rome]({{< relref "/logistica/roma" >}}) and [south Rome]({{< relref "/logistica/roma-sud" >}}).
