E-commerce order fulfilment is the segment of third-party logistics with the most fragmented operating profile: many small orders, one or two lines per order, single-parcel shipments and a share of returns higher than in traditional trade. According to the Netcomm B2c eCommerce Observatory of the Politecnico di Milano, cited by AGI on 27 May 2025, online purchases of products in Italy in 2025 are estimated at about 40.1 billion euros, up 6% on 2024, equal to 11.2% of total product purchases.

For a merchant in Lazio, the choice of warehouse comes down to a few measurable parameters: the order cut-off time, the couriers' collection time, returns management and the road distance from end customers and courier hubs.

## The flow, from order to delivery

The cycle of an e-commerce order handled by a third-party operator consists of seven steps:

1. **receiving goods** from the supplier or manufacturer, with a quantity check, registration of items (SKUs) and put-away;
2. **order integration**: orders from the sales platform reach the operator's WMS (warehouse management system) via a connector, file or IT interface;
3. **picking**, normally by item, with several orders grouped into a single picking round to reduce travel distances;
4. **checking and packing**, with choice of box, void fill, and insertion of documents and promotional materials;
5. **labelling** with the courier's consignment note and generation of the tracking code;
6. **handover to the courier** at the scheduled collection and transmission of tracking data to the platform;
7. **returns management**: receipt, inspection, decision on where the product goes and stock update.

Picking, packing and labelling activities are described on the [handling]({{< relref "/servizi/handling" >}}) service page; the specific needs of the segment are on the [e-commerce]({{< relref "/settori/ecommerce" >}}) sector page.

## Cut-off and collection time

The **cut-off** is the time by which an order received is shipped the same day. It is calculated backwards from the courier's collection time: if the courier calls at 17:00 and the warehouse takes on average two hours to pick, pack and consolidate the parcels of a wave of orders, the cut-off cannot be later than 15:00, and on peak days it has to be brought forward or staffing increased.

Three factors shift the cut-off:

- the **number of collections** per day and the time of the last collection, agreed with each courier;
- **picking productivity**, which depends on how locations are organised: the best-selling items should be placed close to the packing area;
- the **quality of weight and dimension data**, which determine the box and the courier rate without manual measurement.

The service contract should state the cut-off and the indicator used to measure it, such as the percentage of orders received before the cut-off and shipped the same day. On this topic, see the guide to the [third-party logistics contract and KPIs]({{< relref "/approfondimenti/logistica-conto-terzi-pmi-contratto-kpi" >}}).

## Couriers and the geography of Lazio

The merchant can use its own contracts with express couriers or those negotiated by the logistics operator. National couriers collect from the warehouse and sort parcels in their own hubs; for regional B2B deliveries or deliveries of a small number of heavy parcels, the operator can use its own fleet.

## Returns

The consumer's **right of withdrawal** is governed by the Italian Consumer Code (Legislative Decree 206/2005). Article 52 grants 14 days to withdraw without giving reasons, normally from delivery of the goods. Article 56 requires the seller to refund within 14 days of withdrawal, with the option of withholding the refund until the goods are received or proof of their return shipment is provided.

The warehouse must therefore handle returns within timescales compatible with these deadlines:

- **receipt and registration** of the return against the original order number;
- **inspection** of the condition of the product and packaging according to criteria agreed with the merchant;
- **disposition**: return to stock, refurbishment, repacking, sale as second grade or disposal;
- **notification** of the outcome to the platform to release the refund.

A dedicated returns area, separate from saleable stock, prevents uninspected goods from becoming available for orders again.

## Seasonal peaks and capacity

E-commerce concentrates volumes in a few periods of the year, linked to promotions and holidays. For the operator this means planning for additional staff, reserve locations and agreements with couriers for extra collections. For the merchant it means communicating sales forecasts and promotional calendars in advance: an unannounced peak is the most frequent cause of missed cut-offs.
