The province of Latina has 5,446 hectares under actinidia, the kiwifruit plant, making it the largest production area in Lazio. The figure comes from ARSIAL, the regional agency for innovation in agriculture, in its February 2026 report on the crop's chill requirement, based on data from the 2018 AGEA graphic crop plan. Three municipalities account for about 89% of the provincial area: Cisterna di Latina with 2,577 hectares, Aprilia with 1,301 and Latina with 988. Kiwifruit is the flagship product of a fruit and vegetable chain that, from harvest to sale, depends on cold storage and temperature-controlled transport.

## Kiwi Latina PGI

Kiwi Latina was registered as a Protected Geographical Indication under Regulation (EC) No 1486/2004. According to the ARSIAL fact sheet, it covers fruit of Actinidia deliciosa, cultivar Hayward. The production area includes, in the province of Latina, the municipalities of Pontinia, Priverno, Sezze, Sermoneta, Cori, [Cisterna di Latina]({{< relref "/logistica/cisterna-di-latina" >}}), [Aprilia]({{< relref "/logistica/aprilia" >}}) and parts of Sabaudia and [Latina]({{< relref "/logistica/latina" >}}); in the province of Rome it includes, among others, Velletri, Lanuvio, Lariano, Genzano di Roma and parts of Ardea and Pomezia.

The product specification contains requirements with direct consequences for logistics:

- harvesting takes place at a minimum sugar content of 6.2 °Brix, and the fruit is graded as Extra class (over 90 grams) and Class I (over 80 grams);
- kiwifruit is kept in cold stores, in normal or controlled atmosphere, until July of the year following harvest at the latest;
- packing must take place within the geographical production area;
- the maximum yield is 380 quintals per hectare.

Storage until July turns an autumn harvest into supply spread over most of the year, and shifts a significant share of the chain's value to cold storage facilities and packing lines. The ARSIAL fact sheet notes that in the 1970s the Agro Pontino was among the first areas in Italy to host specialised actinidia orchards, and that cold storage and packing facilities have been expanded over time.

## Vine decline and the market

The chain has been through a production crisis. On 20 February 2024 the Lazio regional government asked the Ministry of Agriculture to declare an exceptional event for the damage caused by kiwifruit vine decline («moria del kiwi») in 2023, totalling 215.3 million euros. For the province of Latina the Region estimated 113.1 million euros of damage to production and 41.8 million to farm structures, in the municipalities of Cisterna di Latina, Aprilia, Sabaudia, Pontinia, Priverno, Sezze, Cori, Sermoneta, Terracina, Fondi and Sperlonga. According to the growers cited by the Region, output had fallen by about 50% over the previous year; the regional councillor for agriculture, **Giancarlo Righini**, referred to about 9,000 hectares under cultivation in the region.

At national level, Linkiesta reported in October 2025, citing FreshPlaza, a forecast Italian crop of about 341,000 tonnes for the 2025 season, of which 208,000 tonnes of green kiwifruit and 128,000 tonnes of gold-fleshed kiwifruit. The same article cites figures from Zespri, which has 4,110 hectares in Italy dedicated to its kiwifruit and plans to reach 7,520 hectares by 2031. The growth of gold kiwifruit, which has different seasons and storage requirements from Hayward, increases the range of products handled by the same cold storage facilities.

## Fresh produce, greenhouses and fresh-cut

The Agro Pontino grows vegetables both in open fields and under glass, but up-to-date provincial figures on the area under protected cultivation have not been published by a primary source. The Fondi wholesale food market ([Fondi]({{< relref "/logistica/fondi" >}}), MOF) states that its catchment area has 20,000 cultivated hectares, of which 4,500 under protected cultivation.

Part of the vegetable output goes to the fresh-cut segment (known in Italy as «IV gamma»): vegetables washed, cut and packed ready to eat. Cooperatives in this segment operate in the province, such as San Lidano Società Cooperativa Agricola, based in Sezze. No recent verified figures are available on Pontine volumes; nationally, according to Unione Italiana Food on Circana data reported by Linkiesta, ready-to-eat vegetables had sales of 1.054 billion euros in 2023, and in the first half of 2024 sales exceeded 530 million euros with 406 million packs. For logistics purposes, fresh-cut produce is a packaged fresh product with a short shelf life that requires the temperature to be maintained from the packing line to the point of sale.

## Agri-food exports

According to the Frosinone-Latina Chamber of Commerce report based on ISTAT data, in 2025 the province of Latina exported agricultural products worth 263 million euros, up 4.3%, equal to 2.5% of provincial exports. Food products and beverages reached 339 million euros (+9.1%) and are the third manufacturing sector by exports after pharmaceuticals and metals. The Chamber notes that Pontine food processing has consolidated its growth over the last three years.

## Cold chain logistics in the area

The Pontine cold chain currently relies on three types of facility: the cold stores of farms and cooperatives, the platforms of the wholesale markets and the temperature-controlled warehouses of logistics operators. The MOF in Fondi states that it has a refrigeration plant with a capacity of up to 50,000 quintals and two temperature-controlled logistics platforms totalling 7,000 square metres, with 38 loading bays.

For transport, the reference for international shipments of perishable foodstuffs is the ATP Agreement, adopted in Geneva on 1 September 1970 and in force since 1976 according to the United Nations Economic Commission for Europe (UNECE): it sets the technical standards for special equipment and the transport temperatures for chilled, frozen and deep-frozen products. For sea freight, the Strategic Development Plan of the Lazio ZLS (Simplified Logistics Zone) identifies refrigerated agri-food as one of the specialisations of the port of Civitavecchia.

The Region has also set a direction for rail. The regional mobility, transport and logistics plan (PRMTL) provides for bringing into operation the Latina Scalo rail terminal, currently not in use, with a specialisation as a «cold terminal» for the agri-food and chemical-pharmaceutical chains. The Plan reports that the local production system of Pontine agri-food considers at least one logistics platform for consolidation, storage and distribution of products to be necessary, and that operators regard the Latina Scalo terminal as one of the most suitable sites, given its proximity to Cisterna di Latina, Sermoneta, Sezze and Latina.

## The Latina Scalo site

The warehouse of Consorzio Logistico Latina Scalo, in Via della Dinamica, has 19,000 square metres of covered space with temperature-controlled storage areas, covered loading and unloading bays and yards for articulated lorries, and offers [storage]({{< relref "/servizi/stoccaggio" >}}), [cross-docking and order picking]({{< relref "/servizi/handling" >}}) and [road distribution]({{< relref "/servizi/trasporto" >}}) with a dedicated fleet. By road, the site is about 14.8 kilometres from Cisterna di Latina, 33.5 kilometres from Aprilia and 8.4 kilometres from the centre of Latina, the three municipalities with the largest area under actinidia. Information on the sectors served is on the [sectors]({{< relref "/settori" >}}) page.
