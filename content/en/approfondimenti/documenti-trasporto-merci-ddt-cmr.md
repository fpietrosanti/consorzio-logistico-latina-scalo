A road shipment from the warehouse to the customer travels with several documents, each with a different function. The **transport document (DDT, documento di trasporto)** has a tax function and evidences the movement of goods for VAT purposes. The **consignment note**, domestic or international (CMR), evidences the contract of carriage and governs relations with the carrier. **Incoterms** are not a document but contractual rules which, when referred to in the sales contract, establish where costs and risks pass. Confusing these levels is the most frequent cause of disputes after damage or delay.

## The DDT: tax function

The transport document replaced the former accompanying note (bolla di accompagnamento) with Presidential Decree (DPR) No 472 of 14 August 1996. Article 1(3) sets its minimum content:

- sequential number and date;
- details of the seller, the buyer and any carrier;
- description of the nature, quality and quantity of the goods.

The DDT is not mandatory in every case. It serves two main purposes.

The first is **deferred invoicing**: Article 21(4) of DPR 633/1972 allows a single invoice to be issued for a month's deliveries, by the 15th of the following month, provided each delivery is documented by a DDT or equivalent document.

The second concerns the **presumptions of sale and purchase** under DPR 441/1997. Goods leaving the premises where a business operates are presumed to have been sold, unless proven otherwise. When goods are transferred to a third-party warehouse, to a contract processor or on approval, a DDT stating the correct reason, for example deposit or processing, is the proof that no sale has taken place. For a client entrusting its stock to a logistics operator, the transfer DDT is therefore an essential document.

The DDT must be issued before transport begins and retained in accordance with the rules for VAT documents. In third-party logistics the operator often issues it on behalf of the client, on agreed forms or numbering sequences: tax responsibility remains with the seller.

## The consignment note in domestic transport

The contract of carriage is governed by Articles 1678 et seq. of the Italian Civil Code. Article 1684 provides that the sender, at the carrier's request, issues a **consignment note** (lettera di vettura) stating the consignee, place of destination, and the nature, weight and quantity of the goods. In road haulage for hire, Legislative Decree 286/2005 (Art. 6) requires the written contract to state, among other things, the details of the carrier and the client, the registration number in the national register of road hauliers (Albo degli autotrasportatori), the type and quantity of goods and the places of taking over and delivery.

Two Civil Code rules directly concern shippers:

- **limit of compensation**: Article 1696 limits the compensation payable by the carrier to 1 euro per kilogram of gross weight of goods lost or damaged in domestic carriage, except in cases of wilful misconduct or gross negligence or where otherwise agreed;
- **reservations**: Article 1698 provides that acceptance of the goods without reservation, with payment of the sums due, extinguishes claims against the carrier, except for loss or damage not detectable on delivery, which must be notified as soon as discovered and no later than eight days after receipt.

For high-value goods the limit of 1 euro per kilogram is almost always lower than the actual loss. The solutions are a declaration of value agreed with the carrier or a goods-in-transit policy paid by the client.

## The transport sheet: abolished since 2015

The **transport sheet** (scheda di trasporto) was introduced by Legislative Decree No 214 of 22 December 2008, which inserted Article 7-bis into Legislative Decree 286/2005, with the obligation to carry it on board in road haulage for hire. Law No 190 of 23 December 2014 (2015 Stability Law), Article 1(247), repealed Article 7-bis and deleted the references to the sheet in the decree. Forms and procedures that still require it refer to a provision no longer in force; it remains useful, however, to carry a copy of the contract or the consignment note on board.

## The CMR in international transport

The **Convention on the Contract for the International Carriage of Goods by Road** (CMR), signed in Geneva on 19 May 1956 and implemented in Italy by Law No 1621 of 6 December 1960, applies to carriage of goods by road for reward where the place of taking over and the place of delivery are in two different countries, at least one of which is a contracting party. The main points:

- the CMR consignment note is made out in three originals, for the sender, the consignee and the carrier (Art. 5);
- compensation for loss or damage is limited to 8.33 units of account (special drawing rights of the International Monetary Fund) per kilogram of gross weight short (Art. 23);
- reservations for non-apparent damage must be sent in writing within seven days of delivery, Sundays and public holidays excepted (Art. 30);
- claims are time-barred after one year, or three in the case of wilful misconduct (Art. 32).

The **Additional Protocol concerning the Electronic Consignment Note** (e-CMR), signed in Geneva on 20 February 2008, allows paper to be replaced with a digital document of equal value. Italy acceded to it by Law No 37 of 8 March 2024. In operational use, the other countries on the route must also have acceded and the parties must use a compliant platform.

## Incoterms 2020

**Incoterms** are rules published by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC); the edition in use is Incoterms 2020. There are eleven rules:

- **seven for any mode of transport**: EXW (Ex Works), FCA (Free Carrier), CPT (Carriage Paid To), CIP (Carriage and Insurance Paid To), DAP (Delivered at Place), DPU (Delivered at Place Unloaded), DDP (Delivered Duty Paid);
- **four for sea and inland waterway transport only**: FAS (Free Alongside Ship), FOB (Free on Board), CFR (Cost and Freight), CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight).

Compared with the 2010 edition, DPU replaced DAT, because the place of delivery can be any place and not only a terminal; under CIP the seller must now provide insurance cover complying with Institute Cargo Clauses (A), while CIF retains the minimum level of Clauses (C); under FCA the parties can agree that a bill of lading with an on-board notation is issued.

Incoterms do not govern the transfer of ownership, the price or the contract of carriage. For shippers sending goods by road from a third-party warehouse, the practical point is another: under EXW, loading onto the buyer's vehicle is, according to the rule, the buyer's responsibility, but in practice it is carried out by the warehouse; it is advisable to clarify in writing who is liable, or to use FCA with the warehouse named as the place of delivery.
